Part 3

Our plan

In one line: lock the foundation first, then pick one analysis and do it well.

1 · Put everything on the 2024 dataset

Right now our site tells two stories

The market snapshot uses the old data (27 posts, 5 salaries, no experience). The skill gap uses the new data (29 posts). The new data is far richer: salary on 13 of the 29 roles and experience on 22. Switching fixes the "experience unavailable" hole and the missing charts in one move, and Step 3 told us to use the new dataset.

Proposed owners: Gabriel builds the clean 2024 file; Haben reruns the snapshot charts on it.

2 · Agree on one definition of "Data Engineer"

The two pages count different jobs

The snapshot counts analysts and scientists too; the skill gap counts only true engineering titles. Pick the strict engineering list (29 roles) as the core so every number on the site describes the same group. The checkpoint asks us to confirm every page matches our scope.

3 · Fill the empty pages

One page left to start

Data Preparation and Career Evaluation now have content. Final Recommendations needs a first draft built from the upskilling plan. Career Evaluation could add a short section on what makes a job attractive and attainable.

4 · Use a scorecard for the final analysis

Option C fits our data best

29 roles is too few to train a trustworthy prediction model. A scorecard that ranks those roles on pay, experience required, remote options, and skill match answers our question directly. As a bonus, we can predict salary across all 1,908 insurance posts (1,204 of them list a salary), which is big enough for a real model.

Proposed owner: Gabriel on the salary model.

5 · Name our limits honestly

It makes us look stronger, not weaker

State Farm posted 15 of the 29 roles, so one company shapes the results. The sample is small. Limitations is one of the 7 required report sections anyway.

6 · Quick Step 1 fixes

Small edits, two checklist items

Industry code 5241 is now named and explained. What remains: turn the source links into two or three sentences each on what they say, cited in APA style.
